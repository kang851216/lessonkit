A 30 minute exam timer that reads from the back of the room

While a test runs, the question you hear most is “how many minutes left?”. Answering it one student at a time breaks the class's concentration. A large timer on the projector settles it.

Open the class timer big digits, an end sound, offline

Why a separate timer beats the wall clock

A wall clock makes students do the subtraction, and that is what keeps them glancing at the wall. A timer counts down for them, so the only thing left in a student's head is the work on the desk. In a 30 minute test, that shows in the final minutes.

A timer also removes you as timekeeper: no interrupting the class to say “ten minutes left”, because the screen already does it.

Features that matter in a classroom

  • Large digits. Size follows the screen, so the display stays readable from the back row.
  • Color change in the last 10 seconds. The digits turn red; students see time is nearly up without a word.
  • Three sounds. The signal is audible even while you move around the room.
  • The screen stays awake. On browsers that support it, the display does not switch off, so the projector never shows a dark screen.
  • The tab title counts too: in another tab, the time left stays visible.

If the test needs more time

Sometimes the questions are harder than expected. Press “+1 min” without stopping the timer: the count continues from the time left instead of starting over. That is calmer than resetting it in front of the class.

Keeping the timer from disturbing the test

Put the projector in a mode that hides notifications, and open the timer in a full window. Everything runs in the browser: no uploads, no account, and once opened it keeps working if the school network drops.

Frequently asked questions

Can this timer be set to 30 minutes?

Yes. Presets are 1, 3, 5, and 10 minutes; for 30, press the 10 minute preset then “+1 min” a few times, or type it in when the manual field is available.

Is there a sound when time runs out?

Yes. It sounds three times, and the digits turn red over the last 10 seconds.

Does the screen stay on while the timer runs?

Yes. Where the browser supports it, the timer requests a wake lock, so the projector stays lit.

Can it be used without internet?

Yes. Once the page has been opened once, the timer runs on the device and keeps working when the connection drops.
