Splitting groups and topics at once: who gets which part

Splitting a class into groups is only half the job. The next question is always the same: which group gets which topic? If the teacher decides, one group feels it was handed the hardest part. If the topic comes from a draw, there is nothing to accuse anyone of — and it takes a minute with the same tool.

Split the groups first then draw the topics

Two ways to hand out groups and topics

The jigsaw way (one topic per group). The class is split into four groups and the material into four parts. Each group goes deep into one part, then explains it to another group. This way every student reads, not only the group leader.

The rotation way (all groups, all topics). Each group starts on a different topic, then moves every 10–15 minutes. Suited to material where the parts connect, such as four stages of one process.

Practical steps: split the groups, then draw the topics

  1. Split the groups first with the group maker (for example 32 students → 4 groups × 8 students).
  2. Prepare the topic list, one topic per line, on the name wheel page.
  3. Spin the wheel once per group in group order, and delete each topic once it has been taken.
  4. Write the result down on the board, or keep a screenshot before the activity starts.

Mistakes that make topic assignment fall apart

  • Topics announced before the groups exist. The groups then fight over the one that looks easiest.
  • No record of the draw. When a group forgets its topic, the teacher draws again and the result changes.
  • More topics than groups. The leftover topic leaves one group doing two parts at once.

Saving and sharing the result

The result can be downloaded as an image to stick on the board, or as a CSV file to open in Excel. Once the topics are drawn, write the “group → topic” pair in the right-hand column of that file, then send it to the class chat. Students who were absent still know their group and their topic.

Do not add topics without adding time. The most common reason a rotation lesson fails is a teacher chasing four topics in 30 minutes: the last group always runs out of time, and its topic is never discussed.

Frequently asked questions

Can the topics be drawn with the same tool?

Yes. The name wheel does not distinguish student names from topics: paste the topic list as the list of names, then spin once per group.

What if there are more topics than groups?

Merge two light topics into one, or add a group. Topics that are never drawn usually end up not being done at all.

Can the group result be shared with the class?

Yes. The result can be downloaded as a PNG or CSV file, then sent to the class chat so every student knows their group.
