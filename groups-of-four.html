Groups of four: split a class into groups of 4

Four is usually the most comfortable size for group work: varied enough to divide the jobs, small enough that everyone gets to speak. The trouble is that “pick your own group” always ends the same way — a cluster of close friends, and whoever is left over feeling unwanted.

Split the groups now 2–10 groups, roles included

Work out the number of groups first

Divide the number of students by four, then round. The cases that come up most often: 32 students → 8 groups, 28 students → 7 groups, 36 students → 9 groups.

If the result is not a whole number, do not force every group to hold exactly four. For 30 students, the sensible choices are 7 groups (two of them with 5 students) or 8 groups (two with 3 students). A difference of one student does not matter; what matters is that nobody is left without a group.

Why random grouping beats letting students choose

Random grouping removes two problems at once: groups that are too alike, and the students who are always left over at the end. When the groups come from a draw, nobody can blame a classmate for not being picked — and that is exactly what makes group work easier to start.

If you want to keep the ability range balanced, a method that still feels fair is to draw inside two separate lists (for example students who need support and students who can lead), then combine the results. Still random, but the mix is more controlled.

Roles inside a group of four

  • Chair — keeps time and makes sure everyone speaks.
  • Note-taker — writes down what the group decides.
  • Spokesperson — reports the group's result to the class.
  • Member — does their share of the task and checks a classmate's work.

The group maker can assign these roles at random at the same time, so the same student is not always the chair.

Saving the result so you can use it again

The result can be saved as an image to post in the class chat, or as a CSV file to open in Excel if you need to keep the same groups for a whole term. Student names stay on the device and are not sent anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

How do I split 32 students into groups of four?

Paste the 32 names, choose 8 groups, then press Split. Every group will hold exactly 4 students, picked at random.

Is every group the same size?

When the class size does not divide evenly, the difference is at most one student per group. For example, 30 students in 8 groups gives six groups of 4 and two groups of 3.

Can I save the result?

Yes, as a PNG image for the class chat or a CSV file you can open in Excel.

Is student data safe?

Yes. Everything runs in the browser; no list of names is sent to a server.
